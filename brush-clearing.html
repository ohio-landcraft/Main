Brush clearing

Open up overgrown ground without clearing more than you need.

Brush clearing is about making overgrown areas usable and manageable while keeping the trees, screening and natural character that still serve the property.

Tell Us What You WantSee Capabilities
Start with the outcome

What do you want this part of the property to become?

A mowable edge, fence line, view, trail corridor and wooded yard all call for different levels of brush removal.

Sometimes the answer is simple.

Sometimes mowing, cutting or selectively mulching a narrow area gets the result. There is no reason to clear an entire acre when only the edge, corridor or understory is creating the problem.

What we think through

Enough planning to make the work make sense.

  • What the finished space will be used for
  • Which trees, screening or natural features are worth keeping
  • How the cleared area will be maintained afterward
  • Whether future access, drainage or construction should influence the clearing limits
  • What machine size can work efficiently with the least unnecessary disturbance
  • Whether the work should happen in phases or at a better seasonal window
Make every dollar useful

Clear what is in the way — keep what still adds value.

Selective brush work can reduce machine time, disturbance and future maintenance while preserving shade, screening and the character of the property.

The goal is not to make every area look cleared. It is to make the right areas easier to use and maintain.

The right fit for the work

One Landcraft relationship. The right people and equipment for the job.

Rural property work often overlaps several skills. Through our trusted partner network, we can match the scope with the operator, equipment, hauling, materials or specialty experience it actually needs instead of forcing every project through one crew.

Landcraft stays your point of contact.

When a specialty partner is part of the field work, Ohio Landcraft helps define the scope, coordinate the project and keep the larger property goal in view. You should know who is coming, what they are doing and how that work fits the plan.

Knowledge transfer

Leave knowing how to manage the space.

We talk through what is likely to grow back, what areas may need mowing or follow-up, and what simple maintenance can protect the investment.

  • Know which areas are intended to remain natural
  • Understand how often reclaimed areas may need attention
  • Recognize where drainage or erosion should be watched
  • Plan future phases without undoing the work already completed
