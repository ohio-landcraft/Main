The question we ask first

What should the land become after it is cleared?

Clearing is more useful when the end condition is defined first — access, visibility, mowing, building, recreation, drainage or future maintenance.

Whole-property thinking

Look for the cause.

  • Preserve desirable trees and screening
  • Create maintainable edges
  • Avoid unnecessary disturbance
  • Improve access for future work
  • Coordinate clearing with drainage and grading
  • Plan the finished condition before machine work begins
A practical process

Evaluation → priorities → construction

1. Evaluate

We review the visible issue, surrounding grades, runoff, access and how the area is used.

2. Prioritize

We separate what must be corrected from what can be phased or deferred.

3. Build

We match the right equipment and crew to the solution instead of forcing the solution around a machine.
