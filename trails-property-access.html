Trails & property access

Open up the parts of your property you want to reach.

A useful trail or access route should follow the land, shed water and fit the way you actually plan to use it — whether that means walking, an ATV, a compact tractor or occasional equipment access.

Tell Us What You WantSee Capabilities
Start with the outcome

Where do you need to get — and what needs to get there?

A walking loop, hunting access, an ATV trail and a route for a mini excavator are different projects. The width, grade, surface and drainage should match the real use.

The best route is not always the shortest line.

A slightly longer alignment may avoid a wet area, steep grade, major excavation or unnecessary gravel and end up cheaper to build and easier to maintain.

What we think through

Let the terrain do some of the work.

  • Who or what will use the route
  • Grades, side slopes and safe turning space
  • Where runoff naturally wants to cross the trail
  • Which trees and natural features should be preserved
  • Whether native soil is enough or select areas need aggregate
  • How future maintenance can stay simple
Make every dollar useful

Do not build a road where a trail will do.

Access can often be created with selective clearing and light shaping, then reinforced only at wet, steep or high-use locations.

Matching the route and construction level to the actual vehicle or use can avoid unnecessary clearing, aggregate and machine time.

The right fit for the work

One Landcraft relationship. The right people and equipment for the job.

Rural property work often overlaps several skills. Through our trusted partner network, we can match the scope with the operator, equipment, hauling, materials or specialty experience it actually needs instead of forcing every project through one crew.

Landcraft stays your point of contact.

When a specialty partner is part of the field work, Ohio Landcraft helps define the scope, coordinate the project and keep the larger property goal in view. You should know who is coming, what they are doing and how that work fits the plan.

Knowledge transfer

Know how to keep the route working.

We explain where water is intended to leave the trail, which areas may need periodic trimming and what small maintenance steps can prevent larger repairs.

  • Keep culverts, dips and outlets open
  • Watch for wheel ruts that begin to channel water
  • Trim regrowth before the corridor closes back in
  • Add stone only where use and conditions show it is needed
